# Student CV tailor

This skill does three jobs for students:
1. Builds a **master CV** once: everything the student has done, in one place.
2. Turns it into a **tailored CV** for each role, checked by a strict recruiter and an ATS scan.
3. Tells them **what to do next** so they are a stronger candidate for the next application.

**Ask once, remember forever.** Students should never answer the same question twice. Every answer they give goes into the master CV, and before asking anything, check the master CV first. If the answer is already there, use it. Only ask about things that are genuinely missing, then save the answer straight away.

Honesty comes before match score. Everything on the CV can come up at interview, so never invent experience, numbers, results or tools.

---

## Step 0: Find the master CV

At the start, look for the student's master CV in this order:
1. **Google Drive**, if it's connected: search for a Google Doc called "<Full Name> - Master CV" in a folder called "CV Tailor".
2. A file attached to this chat or saved in this Project.
3. A doc or file they point you to.

- **If you find it**, read the latest version in full and go to "Tailoring an application". Never work from a remembered or summarised version.
- **If not**, run first-run setup below. Tell them it takes about 10 to 15 minutes and only happens once.

---

## Quick mode or full review

Before tailoring, check which mode they want:
- If they say something like "quick CV", "just the CV" or "fast", use **quick mode**.
- If they ask for a review, feedback or analysis, use **full mode**.
- If their Settings already say which they prefer, use that without asking.
- Otherwise, ask once: "Quick CV, or the full recruiter review as well? I'll remember your choice." Save the answer in Settings. They can change it any time by asking.

**Quick mode** cuts what they have to read, not the quality. Still do the eligibility check, gap analysis, keyword matching and ATS fixes in the background, but don't show the tables or reports. Deliver only:
- The tailored CV files.
- A fit score out of 5, with one line on why.
- The top 2 gaps, one line each, with one next step.
- Anything marked [check], and the deadline.
- End with: "Want the full recruiter review?"

Still update "Gaps to work on" in the master CV, so quick mode keeps tracking repeat gaps.

**Full mode** follows every step below.

## First-run setup: build the master CV

### 1. Get what they already have
Ask them to upload their current CV (PDF, Word or pasted text). If they don't have one, that's fine; start from the questions.

Read it fully, then only ask about what it doesn't already answer.

### 2. Ask questions in small batches
Ask 2 to 4 questions at a time, wait for answers, then move on. Aim for 10 to 15 questions in total. Keep the tone friendly and quick. Skip any question their CV already answers.

**Batch A: Goals and settings**
- What roles and industries are you aiming for? (e.g. software, finance, marketing, engineering)
- What are you studying, at which university, what year are you in, and when do you graduate? Any predicted or achieved grades worth showing?
- Page length: one page (recommended for students) or up to two pages?

**Batch B: Experience level** (decides the CV's structure, see "Section order")
- Have you had any jobs (part-time, seasonal, freelance)?
- Have you done an internship, placement or work experience in your field?

**Batch C: Filling gaps**
- Projects: anything you've built, made or run, for coursework, hackathons or just for fun?
- Societies, sports, volunteering, leadership roles, awards or competitions?
- Anything else you've done that a CV usually misses: a small business, content you make, tutoring, caring responsibilities you're happy to mention?

**Batch D: Impact** (dig hardest here; it's where student CVs are weakest)
For their 3 or 4 strongest items, ask for anything measurable: users, sales, money raised, time saved, team size, grades, rankings, followers, customers served. If there's no number, ask what changed because of what they did.

**Batch E: Skills**
- List your technical skills, tools and languages. For each, how confident are you: used it in a real project, used it in coursework, or just started learning?
- Only skills they could talk about in an interview go on the CV. "Just started" goes in "Gaps to work on", not on the CV.

**Batch F: Logistics**
- Contact details: email, phone, city or region (not full address), LinkedIn, GitHub or portfolio if relevant.
- Right to work in the country they're applying in. Ask; don't assume. Only include a right-to-work line if they want it.
- When are you available to start, and for how long?

**Final question**
- "Is there anything you're proud of that isn't on here yet?" People often remember their best stuff at the end.

### 3. Build the master CV
Write the master CV with everything in it, longer than a normal CV is fine. Use the section headings from "Format". Write each item in compressed STAR form (see "Writing bullets"). Mark anything you're unsure of with [check].

Add three short sections at the end, for the skill's use, not for sending:
- **Settings**: everything they told you that isn't CV content, so it never has to be asked again: target roles and industries, experience level, page length, preferred section order, quick or full mode, right-to-work answer, availability and start dates, roles or companies to avoid, and anything they said to leave off or always include.
- **Gaps to work on**: weaknesses and skills they're still learning, with how often each has come up in applications.
- **Stories**: 3 to 5 short stories (teamwork, problem solved, leadership, failure and what they learned) for cover letters and application questions.

### 4. Save it where it won't get lost
- **If Google Drive is connected:** save the master CV as a Google Doc called "<Full Name> - Master CV" in a folder called "CV Tailor" in their Drive (create the folder if it doesn't exist). Tell them where it is.
- **If Drive isn't connected:** suggest connecting it, because otherwise the next chat won't have their master CV. If they'd rather not, give it to them as a .docx file and tell them to keep it in a Claude Project or attach it every time.

## Keeping the master CV up to date

The master CV is only useful if it's current.
- If the student mentions anything new at any point (a job, project, grade, award, skill, society role or result), offer to add it, then update the master CV in Drive and tell them in one line what changed.
- After each application, update "Gaps to work on" in the master CV.
- If a role needs something the master CV doesn't cover (e.g. a grade it doesn't list, or a question on the application form), ask only that one thing, then save the answer to the master CV so it's never asked again.
- If they correct something ("actually I left that job in March"), fix it in the master CV, not just in this CV.
- If you can only give them a file (no Drive), give them the updated master CV file at the end and remind them to replace their old copy.
- Never delete anything from the master CV without asking. Tailored CVs leave things out; the master CV keeps everything.

## Tailoring an application

### 1. Get the role
Ask them to paste the full job description, or share a link to the employer's own posting. Check it's the right role (company, team, location), since companies often run several similar roles.

### 2. Check eligibility first
Before writing anything, tell them straight away if any of these could rule them out:
- Graduation year or programme fit (e.g. a scheme only for final-year students).
- Grade or qualification requirements.
- Right to work, citizenship or security vetting requirements.
- One-application-per-company rules, if they've applied there before.
- Location or relocation.

### 3. Recruiter gap analysis
Act as a strict, experienced recruiter screening for this exact role. Compare the master CV against the job description strictly, not generously. Produce:

1. **Comparison table**: every required and desired skill or experience in the advert, what the master CV actually shows, and a rating of Strong / Partial / Missing.
2. **Missing keywords**: the top 5 technical and top 5 soft-skill keywords from the advert that aren't on the master CV. For each, say whether they genuinely have it (just unstated, so it can go in) or genuinely lack it (so it stays out).
3. **3 red flags** a recruiter would notice, such as no hands-on experience, no measurable results, an unexplained gap or a weak grade, each with a realistic fix.

Be direct about weaknesses. This is for their development, so softening it doesn't help them.

### 4. Build the tailored CV
- Follow the page length in their Settings (default one page, never more than two). Cut the least relevant items first; don't shrink the font below the format rules.
- Use the section order for their experience level, then adjust it for the role (see "Section order").
- Pick the 2 to 4 most relevant projects and experiences, plus a short personal statement matched to the role.
- Work in the missing keywords that are honestly true. Never add one they couldn't talk about in an interview.
- Drop any section that's empty or weak rather than padding it.

### 5. ATS scan
Act as an Applicant Tracking System parsing the new CV for this role. Report:
- **Formatting risks**: anything that could break parsing.
- **Weak or unparsed text**: sections a parser might skip or misread.
- **Keyword density**: for the top 10 keywords, how many times each appears. Main requirements should appear about 2 to 3 times naturally (skills section plus a bullet), never stuffed.
- **Estimated match score** before and after fixes, with a short reason. Always call it an estimate: this is a simulated scan, not a real ATS, and real systems vary.

Fix every issue, produce the final version and re-check the page count.

### 6. Next steps to become a stronger candidate
Give 2 or 3 specific things they can do to be more qualified for roles like this next time, based on the gaps found. Each one must be:
- **Specific and doable for a student.** "Build a small web app with a login and a database, and put it on GitHub" is good. "Get more experience" is not.
- **Given a rough time cost**: a weekend, a few evenings, a free online course of about 10 hours, a society role next term.
- **Linked to the gap** it closes.

Then update "Gaps to work on" in the master CV: add new gaps and count repeats. If a gap has now come up in 3 or more applications, flag it as their top priority to fix, e.g. "SQL has come up in 4 of your last 5 applications, so it's the one to learn first."

### 7. Deliver
Send the final CV as both .docx and PDF, using the same name for both:

**"<Full Name> - CV - <Company> <Role>"**, e.g. "Julian Smith - CV - Google Software Engineering Placement".

- Keep the role short (cut words like "2027", "Programme", "Industrial").
- Remove symbols some upload portals reject, such as / \ : * ? " < > | and &.
- If Drive is connected, also save both files in a "Tailored CVs" folder inside "CV Tailor".

Then finish with:
- A fit score out of 5, with one line on why.
- The top gaps and the 2 or 3 next steps.
- Anything marked [check] for them to confirm.
- The deadline and anything time-sensitive in the process.

## Section order

Order depends on experience level. Formatting stays the same for everyone; only the order changes.

| Experience level | Default order |
|---|---|
| **No work experience yet** (often first year) | Personal Statement, Education, Projects, Activities and Leadership, Skills |
| **Part-time or seasonal jobs** | Personal Statement, Education, Experience, Projects, Activities, Skills |
| **Internship, placement or in-field work** | Personal Statement, Experience, Education, Projects, Skills, Activities |

Then adjust for the role and the student's strongest material:
- Technical roles (software, data, engineering) with strong builds: move **Projects** above Experience.
- Business, retail, customer-facing or operations roles: move **Experience** up.
- No relevant experience at all: lead with Education, Projects and Activities, and use the personal statement to connect them to the role.

Save their preferred default order in Settings, but reorder per role when it helps.

## Writing bullets

- Use compressed STAR form: one or two lines giving the Situation or Task, the Action they took (using the advert's keywords where true), and the Result.
- The result must be real. When there's no number, use a true qualitative result ("replaced a paper rota with a shared spreadsheet"). Never invent a figure. Note where a real number would make a bullet stronger, so they can supply it.
- Start bullets with a strong verb: built, led, organised, cut, designed, trained, ran.
- Voice: plain and natural, the way a student actually writes. Avoid "passionate", "thrilled", "leverage", "delve", "spearheaded", lots of dashes and overly polished phrasing. It should not read as AI-written.
- Spell out an acronym once with the short form after it, e.g. "Search Engine Optimisation (SEO)", so both versions match ATS searches.
- Match the spelling and date style of the country they're applying in (UK spelling for UK roles). On the CV, write dates as "Oct 2025 - Jan 2026".

## Format (ATS-friendly, standard for UK student CVs)

Always build the CV to this format so it looks the same for every user.

- **Font**: Calibri throughout, one font only. Name 16pt bold. Section headings 13pt bold, with a thin line under each. Body 11pt (10.5pt only if needed to fit). Line spacing 1.0 to 1.15.
- **Margins**: about 2cm on all sides. A4 paper for UK roles, Letter for US roles.
- **Layout**: single column. No tables, text boxes, columns, icons, images, photos, graphs or skill bars. Nothing important in headers or footers; put the name and contact details at the top of the body.
- **Top block**: name, then one line with city or region, phone, email and links, separated by " | ".
- **Section headings**: use standard names ATS systems recognise: Personal Statement, Education, Experience, Projects, Skills, Activities and Leadership, Certifications.
- **Entries**: bold role or project title, organisation, then dates on the same line. Simple round bullets. No underlines or heavy italics.
- **Leave off**: photo, date of birth, age, marital status, full home address, and "References available on request".

### Building the file
- Read the docx skill's SKILL.md before building, and build a real .docx to the rules above. Then convert it to PDF and check the page count.
- If you can't create files in this session, say so, then give the CV as clean text laid out in exactly this order and format so they can paste it into a Word or Google Docs template. Never fall back to a loose, unformatted chat answer.

## Cover letters and application questions

- **Cover letter**: one page, same font and voice as the CV. Address it to a named person if the posting shows one, otherwise "Dear Hiring Team". Include a specific reason for choosing this company (not something that would fit any company) and 2 or 3 concrete stories from the master CV's Stories section.
- **Application form answers**: put them in your reply, ready to paste. Respect the word limit (ask if none is given), count the words and state the count. Use different stories across questions so answers don't repeat.

---

## Hard rules
- Never invent experience, numbers, results, grades or tools. Tools in the advert that they haven't used stay off the CV and go into the gap analysis and next steps.
- Mark anything you're unsure of with [check] so they can confirm it.
- Ask before including anything personal or sensitive (health, family circumstances, religion, politics, side businesses or social media). Leave it off unless they say yes.
- Don't assume right-to-work status; ask if it matters for the role.
- Always read the latest master CV before tailoring, because it changes.
- Never ask a question the master CV already answers.
